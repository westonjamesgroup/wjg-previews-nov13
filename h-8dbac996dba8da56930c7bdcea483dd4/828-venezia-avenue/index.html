Venice  ·  Modern Residence

828 Venezia Avenue

For Sale  ·  $3,995,000

Tucked behind a private gate on Venezia Avenue, just blocks from Abbot Kinney Boulevard, this architectural residence by the late Patrick Killen, AIA, is defined by its interplay of natural light, expansive volumes, and seamless connection to the outdoors.

Central to the design is an open upper-level void that draws natural light into the living spaces below, visually connecting both storeys. Expansive walls of glass and a series of outdoor terraces extend the living experience beyond the interior, from the ground-level patios to the rooftop deck.

The main level unfolds as a continuous living environment, where polished concrete, warm wood, and floor-to-ceiling glass establish a refined material palette. A fireplace anchors the living area, while the adjoining dining space opens to patios at the front and side of the residence. The kitchen centers around a generous island with an integrated wine refrigerator, complemented by a separate office offering privacy from the principal living spaces.

Upstairs, three en-suite bedrooms provide comfortable, private accommodations. The primary suite features a walk-in closet and a beautifully appointed bathroom with a steam shower and freestanding soaking tub. Two balconies, a dedicated laundry area, and additional linen storage complete the level. Above, a rooftop deck surrounded by palms offers a secluded setting for outdoor entertaining and relaxation.

Beyond the yard, a detached two-car garage with a glazed sliding door offers versatility for use as a creative studio, fitness space, or additional workspace.

Thoughtfully conceived and distinctly Californian, the residence reflects Killen’s sensitivity to proportion, light, and indoor-outdoor living, set within the vibrant architectural landscape of Venice.

Patrick Killen, AIA

The Architect

Killen founded Studio 9one2 in Manhattan Beach and, from the 1980s, was one of a handful of architects bringing a serious Modernism to the South Bay's narrow coastal lots. His Sherin House, on the Strand, took its cues from the lifeguard towers and has been called bold and unprecedented. The work was collected in books such as The New 100 Houses and The World’s Best Beach Houses.
